Optimizing Corn Silage Fermentation & Reducing Starch Loss in High-Yield Dairy Herds

Dr. Frank Morales, Ph.D.
Lead Ruminant Forage Specialist
Nov 02, 2026 7 min read 1,420 views
In This Article

  • 2. Bunker Packing Density
  • 3. Bacterial Inoculants Role
  • 4. Starch Degradability & Fermentation
  • 5. Management Takeaways
Share Article

Corn silage represents between 40% and 65% of the total dry matter intake on most commercial dairy operations. Yet, up to 15% of total harvested energy is routinely lost to improper bunker pack density, delayed oxygen purging, and uncontrolled heating at the bunker face.

1. Target Moisture at Harvest Timing

Harvesting whole-plant corn forage at the correct whole-plant moisture (between 32% and 35% dry matter) is the single most critical factor for anaerobic compaction. Harvesting above 38% dry matter impedes roller mill grain processing and traps interstitial oxygen, creating ideal breeding conditions for wild yeasts and molds.

Conversely, harvesting below 30% dry matter results in extensive effluent seepage, leaching soluble proteins and fermentable sugars away from the bunker into holding lagoons.

Rule of Thumb:

Always utilize microwave or Koster moisture testing in the field rather than relying solely on kernel milk-line inspection.

2. Bunker Packing Density & Layer Thickness

Achieving a minimum bulk packing density of 240 kg of dry matter per cubic meter (15 lbs DM/cu. ft) requires deliberate tractor weight budgeting. For every 100 tons of fresh forage brought to the bunker per hour, a minimum of 40 tons of packing tractor weight must maintain continuous tire passes over layers no thicker than 15 cm (6 inches).

3. Homofermentative vs. Heterofermentative Inoculants

Biological inoculants provide two distinct bacterial functions depending on bunker history:

  • Homofermentative strains (e.g., Lactobacillus plantarum): Produce pure lactic acid to rapidly drop bunker pH below 4.0 within 72 hours, saving valuable fermentable dry matter.
  • Heterofermentative strains (e.g., Lactobacillus buchneri): Synthesize moderate levels of acetic acid, which directly inhibits the growth of wild yeasts and molds upon exposure to air at the feeding face.

4. Starch Degradability & Fermentation Aging

The hydrophobic prolamin zein protein matrix surrounding corn starch granules degrades slowly in fermented silage. Testing reveals that whole-plant corn silage fermented for 120 days yields up to 8% higher rumen starch digestibility (7-hour in situ) compared to fresh 30-day silage.

5. Actionable Management Takeaways

  1. Measure chopper processor kernel clearance (1.5mm - 2.0mm) every 4 hours during harvest.
  2. Seal the bunker immediately upon completion using 2-step oxygen barrier barrier film (such as Silostop).
  3. Maintain a clean, straight bunker face using a defacer rather than digging with an end-loader bucket.
